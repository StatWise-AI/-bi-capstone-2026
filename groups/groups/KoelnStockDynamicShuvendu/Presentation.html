01
01 The Business Problem

Two opposite failures, at the same time.

A regional distribution center runs 500 products across 8 categories. Some run out and cost the business sales. Others sit overstocked and tie up cash and shelf space. Both are happening at once, and nobody could see, product by product, which problem they were looking at.

What the business asked for

Classify every product as High, Medium, or Low risk of stocking out, then use that label to decide:

"Which products to reorder first, which suppliers to renegotiate with, and where to hold more safety stock."

Source: the dataset's own Use_Case_Documentation sheet, business goal statement.

03 The Semantic Model

23 DAX measures, one calculated column.

Power BI measures are reusable formulas: write the calculation once, and every chart, card, and slicer recalculates it instantly. Three families cover everything the dashboard shows.

Risk & Action

High / Medium / Low Risk SKUs - count of products in each tier.

Top Priority Escalations = High Risk AND top-value (ABC "A") products.

Below Reorder Point = CALCULATE(COUNTROWS, Stock < Reorder Point).

Financial & Operational

Total Stock Value = SUMX(stock × unit cost) across all 500 SKUs.

Total Storage Space, Avg Holding Cost %, Avg Turnover Rate - the cost side of carrying inventory.

Supplier & Demand

Root Cause Review SKUs = more than 5 stockouts last year.

Secondary Supplier Candidates = lead time > 30 days AND above-average demand.

Reorder_Gap (column) = Reorder Point minus Current Stock.

Deliberate exclusion: the pre-computed Risk_Score and Current_Stock_Coverage_Days columns were kept out of anything that predicts risk, since the documentation implies Risk_Score already encodes the answer - using it as an input would be circular.

08
08 Decision Rules We Encoded

Every threshold on the dashboard came from the brief, not a guess.

Risk Score ≥ 60

Immediate action: emergency reorder, safety stock up 50%, monitored daily.

35 ≤ Risk Score < 60

Proactive management: standard reorder schedule, reviewed weekly.

Risk Score < 35

Routine operations: normal process, reviewed monthly.

High Risk + ABC "A"

Escalated as top priority. 30 SKUs qualify today.

>5 stockouts last year

Flagged for root cause review. 245 SKUs qualify today.

Lead time >30 days + high demand

Flagged for a second supplier. 54 SKUs qualify today.

09 Bonus · Machine Learning Validation

Could a model actually predict the risk label the dashboard reports?

A companion notebook (runnable in Google Colab) trains four classifiers on the 25 business features the brief specifies, deliberately holding out Risk_Score to avoid teaching the model the answer.

Test-set result vs. the brief's own targets

MetricTargetAchievedMet?
Overall accuracy> 85%80.7%No
High Risk precision> 80%89.7%Yes

Medium Risk is the hardest class to separate (72.9% F1) - the honest finding is that the model is very good at catching the highest-stakes case, even where its overall accuracy falls short.

10 The Story The Data Tells

Four pages, one narrative arc.

OVERVIEW

38.8% of the catalog is already High Risk, and it isn't evenly spread across categories.

RISK & ACTION

30 of those are also top-value stock - the list that should be worked first, today.

INVENTORY HEALTH

Service level sits 12.8 points under target, while excess stock is, right now, not the problem.

SUPPLIER & DEMAND

245 SKUs have repeat stockouts, and 54 are tied to slow suppliers carrying strong demand.

The four pages don't repeat one chart four ways - each one answers a different question a manager would ask next: what's wrong, what do I do, what's it costing, and why is it happening.
